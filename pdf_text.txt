The Urban Environmental Intelligence Challenge  
Data Source  
Data Source:  OpenAQ Global Air Quality API  (Fetch parameters for 100 stations for the 
entire year 2025).  
Scenario  
You are the Lead Data Architect for a Smart City initiative. You are working with a multi -
gigabyte dataset collected from 100 global sensor nodes . Each node records hourly values 
for PM2.5, PM10, NO2, Ozone, Temperature, and Humidity throughout the year 2025.  
Objective  
Your goal is to build a diagnostic engine that can identify environmental anomalies. The 
engine will be evaluated on your ability to choose visualizations that maximize data -ink 
ratio  and minimize scale distortion . 
Task 1: The Dimensionality Challenge (25%)  
You are required to analyze the relationships among six different environmental variables 
monitored by 100 sensors.  
• The Problem:  Standard scatter plots are ineffective due to the high number of 
dimensions and data points, making the visualization unreadable.  
• The Task:  Apply a Dimensionality Reduction  technique to project the six -
dimensional data into two dimensions.  (Hint : First standardize the six environmental 
variables, then apply a dimensionality reduction technique that transforms correlated 
features into a smaller set of new axes. After projecting the data into two dimensions, 
analyze the loadings of each axis to deter mine which variables contribute most to the 
separation between Industrial and Residential zones. ) 
• The Analysis:  Present a visualization that highlights how city zones (Industrial vs. 
Residential) cluster together. Provide justification for your choice of dimensionality 
reduction method, and explain what the "loadings" of your axes reveal about the main 
drivers of u rban pollution.  
Task 2: High -Density Temporal Analysis (25%)  
The Mayor needs to review instances where "Health Threshold Violations" (PM2.5 > 35 
µg/m³) have occurred across all 100 sensors simultaneously  to determine which 
neighborhoods consistently exceed safe limits.  
• The Problem:  A standard line chart with 100 lines results in excessive chart clutter.  
• The Task:  Choose and implement a high -density temporal visualization method that 
enables the comparison of 100 time -series within a compact space, avoiding 
overplotting.  
• The Analysis:  Use the chosen visualization to identify the "Periodic Signature" of 
pollution events. Determine if they are driven by daily (24 -hour traffic cycles) or 
monthly (30 -day seasonal shifts) patterns.  
Task 3: Distribution Modeling & Tail Integrity (25%)  
You are to report the probability of "Extreme Hazard" events, specifically when PM2.5 levels 
exceed 200 µg/m³.  
• The Problem:  Traditional histograms may obscure rare, extreme values due to bin 
size choices, potentially hiding the "long tail."  
• The Task:  Produce two different distribution plots for a selected industrial zone. One 
plot should be optimized to reveal peaks , and the other to reveal tails. 
• The Analysis:  Using these plots, determine the 99th percentile of pollution levels. 
Provide a technical justification for which plot offers a more "honest" depiction of 
these rare, hazardous events.  
Task 4: The Visual Integrity Audit (25%)  
You have been presented with a proposal to use a 3D bar chart to display Pollution vs. 
Population Density vs. Region.  
• The Task:  Decide whether to accept or reject this proposal based on the Lie Factor  
and Data -Ink Ratio  principles.  
• The Task:  If you reject the proposal, implement either a Bivariate Mapping  solution 
or a Small Multiples  approach that accurately allows for comparison of these three 
variables.  
• The Analysis:  Justify your selection of a color scale (Sequential vs. Rainbow) with 
respect to human perception of luminance.  
Technical Constraints (Strict Enforcement)  
1. Big Data Handling : After assignment 1 you are fully aware how to handle Big Data. 
Apply those concepts here too.  
2. No Graphical Ducks:  Avoid 3D effects, shadows, or unnecessary grids in all 
visualizations.  
3. Reproducibility:  Submit your work as a modular Python (.py) pipeline. Submissions 
using Jupyter Notebooks will be penalized.
